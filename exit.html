What I Took Away

Lessons from GED108 Art Appreciation

01 Look twice

Honestly I almost missed Icarus in that painting. Like he’s literally just two tiny legs in the corner and everyone else is just doing their normal thing. I only found him cause Bruegel kinda forces you to look around for him. After that I stopped just scrolling past stuff, I actually try to look at what the artist wants me to see and what they want me to miss.

02 Culture changes the picture

The Water Margin print and the George Washington painting both show a “hero” but they feel so different. One is full of crashing water and action and the other is just Washington standing there looking serious. At first I thought the Washington one was boring, but it made me realize every culture shows strength in its own way. Neither one is wrong, they just come from different places.

03 Art answers its time

I didn’t expect Op Art to be connected to so much stuff happening back then. It was the 1960s, there was the Vietnam War, civil rights problems, and space tech was blowing up too. So the artists were messing with the way our eyes see things while the whole world was changing. It made me think art isn’t just made randomly, it kinda carries whatever was going on around the artist.

04 Making it myself

Recreating The Thinker was harder than I thought. You have to think about the pose, the lighting, the background, even where your hand goes. I added a blindfold to show how we get overloaded by info online, like social media and fake news, so you’re thinking hard but you can’t really see what’s real. Just one small change and the whole meaning changed, and I learned more from doing it than from just reading about it.

05 Feelings need no translation

Nighthawks is from 1940s America, which is super far from where I live, but I still got it right away. Four people in a diner at night, sitting close but kinda in their own worlds. I think most people have felt that before. It showed me that you don’t need words for that, the light and the shadows and the framing already say it, so people from different cultures can still feel the same thing.

06 Art is for everyone

I got to visit the Met, the Musée d’Orsay and the Tokyo National Museum without leaving my room, which is kinda crazy. Before this I thought museums were just dusty places with old stuff. Now I see them as places where all these different people’s lives and feelings are kept alive. Op Art was also supposed to be for everybody, not just people who go to galleries, so that felt right to me.

A Thank You

Thank you to my teacher for letting us explore so many different kinds of art. I came in thinking art history was just dates and old paintings. Now I look twice at everything, from a painting in a museum to a diner at night. I'm really grateful I got to make things and not just memorize them.
